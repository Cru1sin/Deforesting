## Venue Requirements

Nature Communications publishes Articles that make an important, technically convincing advance of interest beyond a narrow specialist audience. This manuscript should therefore be framed as a general measurement-to-supervision method for condition-based decision support, with heat-pump defrosting as the test case, rather than as a controller-performance report. The paper should use a concise, non-technical abstract; a focused Introduction; evidence-led Results; a limitations-aware Discussion; and sufficiently detailed Methods. Data- and code-availability statements, source data for figures, transparent statistical reporting, and reproducible partitioning and evaluation procedures are essential. Initial presentation should be readable across energy systems, sensing and machine-learning communities; exact submission-format details should be rechecked against the journal's current author guide before submission.

## Review Criteria

Editors and reviewers are likely to ask whether the work establishes a substantial conceptual advance over optimal-timing, frost-tracking and image-based defrost studies already represented by HP-001–HP-006. They will assess whether the cost functional is physically defensible, whether its fixed-ticket and offline-anchor assumptions are explicit, and whether unsmoothed inputs and sensitivity analyses make the derived states auditable. For the RGB claim, experiment/date-held-out splitting, independence at cycle level, locked model and camera selection, uncertainty intervals, leakage controls, class coverage and robustness across operating conditions will be decisive. Reviewers will also test whether every deployment or causal inference is excluded unless supported by intervention data.

## Accepted Paper Patterns

One suitable pattern is **problem → measurement gap → methodological bridge → independent validation**: conventional timing objectives and visual sensing exist separately; this study converts a cycle-specific empirical cost surface into pointwise regret labels, then tests whether RGB images recover those labels in unseen experiments. A second pattern is **primary result → benchmark → robustness → boundary**: establish the locked held-out RGB result, compare high-confidence binary with pre/near/post classification and time-only baselines, test camera and threshold sensitivity, then quantify abstention or near-optimal ambiguity and state the retrospective limits.

## Constraints for This Paper

The present evidence supports a retrospective method and proof of concept only. Claims must remain conditional on the observed fixed-duration defrost ticket and must not imply causal global optimality, prospective energy savings, occupant comfort improvement or deployable online operation. The exact argmin is weakly identified in many cycles, so pointwise regret and coverage should carry the narrative. The primary RGB claim remains provisional until full-cohort, cycle/experiment-held-out evaluation is locked; the current two-cycle local validation cannot support journal-level generalization. Novelty should be restricted to the auditable bridge from raw operational cost to ambiguity-aware RGB supervision, not first optimal timing or first image defrost control. RGB must also outperform or complement a cycle-time-only baseline before being described as decision-informative.
